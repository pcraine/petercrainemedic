Background & Experience

About Me

Practising Emergency Ambulance Crew · St John Ambulance · North West England

“Most people see a dance competition or a road race. I see the patients who might need me — and I make sure I'm ready for every one of them.”

I'm a practising Emergency Ambulance Crew (EAC) member and FREC 3-certified pre-hospital practitioner with St John Ambulance, deployed operationally across the North West at sporting events, national competitions, music venues, community events and blue light emergency response.

Clinical Skills Profile
Clinical Background

My clinical training covers the full pre-hospital emergency care spectrum. I am competent in advanced airway management using OPA, NPA and iGel adjuncts, bag-valve-mask ventilation, capnography, 12-lead ECG acquisition and interpretation, haemorrhage control, spinal immobilisation with cervical collar and head blocks, traction splinting, pelvic binder application, blood glucose monitoring, and major incident management using the METHANE and JESIP frameworks.

I administer medications to full EAC scope — including adrenaline (IM), salbutamol (nebuliser), entonox, methoxyflurane (Penthrox), naloxone (IM), hydrocortisone (IM), GTN, glucagon (IM), ipratropium bromide, aspirin, paracetamol, ibuprofen, loratadine, glucose gel and oral rehydration salts. Every patient contact is documented to PRF standard and handed over using ATMIST and SBAR protocols to receiving NWAS crews, including formal pre-alert where required.

I operate within full SJA clinical governance — maintaining competence to JRCALC+ guidelines, completing mandatory CPD, maintaining a personal development portfolio, and actively managing skill fade. Every piece of patient-related documentation is completed accurately and submitted through the correct channels. That is not optional. It is what clinical accountability looks like.

When deployed alongside less experienced crew, I act as senior clinician — holding clinical responsibility for the patient, coaching and guiding junior grades as required. At EAC level I am qualified to act as lead clinician on a treatment centre and to lead clinically complex out-of-hospital patient scenarios.

I hold PCV/D1, Category C and CERAD Level 3 blue light driving authority and operate response vehicles under full driving authority.

First Aid Training

I am a qualified first aid trainer holding CTLLS Level 4 (City & Guilds) and AAVRA Assessor credentials, with 200+ learners trained across FAW, EFAW, BLS, AED Awareness and bespoke programmes in corporate, community and clinical settings.

The difference between my training and a standard course: I bring genuine active clinical context. When I explain an anaphylaxis protocol, I've administered adrenaline in the field. That matters to learners.

Community Service

Before my clinical career I founded and operated a community foodbank in Blackburn. I serve as a Police Volunteer with Lancashire Constabulary's Mental Health Diagnostic Team, and I stand as Cenotaph Guard of Honour each Remembrance Sunday.

Clinical Reality

On Deployment

What actually happens when an EAC is deployed — the clinical standard behind every event.

Professional event medical cover for any event — running races, dance festivals, corporate days, community events and everything in between. Every deployment is different. The venue changes, the crowd changes, the risk profile changes. What doesn't change is the clinical standard. As a FREC 3-qualified EAC, I operate to full EAC scope on every duty — assessing patients, making clinical decisions, administering medications, handing over to NWAS — regardless of whether I'm on a ballroom floor or a rural race route.

EAC Scope of Practice

What you're getting when you book an EAC — not a first-aider in a green jacket
Clinical Assessment
Full primary & secondary survey, cABCDE adult & paediatric, vital signs monitoring, NEWS2 early warning score, FAST stroke assessment, AMTS, GCS, pupil assessment, clinical decision-making to EAC standard
Airway & Breathing
OPA, NPA, iGel supraglottic airway, BVM ventilation (single & two-person), mechanical & manual suction, oxygen therapy, capnography, airway emergency management including laryngectomy/tracheostomy patients
Medications
19 medications within EAC scope — GSL bag: aspirin, paracetamol, ibuprofen, loratadine, glucose gel, oral rehydration salts, Penthrox, entonox, oxygen. Intermediate Medicines Bag: adrenaline 1:1,000 (IM), naloxone (IM), salbutamol (NEB), hydrocortisone (IM), glucagon (IM), GTN (SL), ipratropium bromide (NEB), plus patient's own: adrenaline auto-injector, GTN, salbutamol inhaler. All administered per SJA Medication Administration Cross Check protocol.
Trauma & Immobilisation
Haemorrhage control, haemostatic gauze, wound packing, tourniquet (CAT/Tourni-Key), pelvic binder, traction splinting, spinal immobilisation (cervical collar & head blocks, MILS), crash helmet removal, open fracture management, soft tissue injuries, orthopaedic stretcher
Monitoring
12-lead ECG acquisition & interpretation (STEMI, arrhythmia recognition), pulse oximetry, blood pressure, blood glucose, capnography for cardiac arrest & critically unwell
Scene & Incident Management
Dynamic risk assessment, scene safety, CBRNe awareness, METHANE and JESIP principles, Major Incident Triage Tool, Crew Resource Management, pre-alert to receiving hospital (ATMIST/ASHICE), PPCI & stroke pathway awareness
Patient Welfare & Governance
Safeguarding referrals (all ages), Mental Capacity Act assessment, valid consent (including under-18s), DNACPR/ReSPECT documentation awareness, capacity assessment, end of life and bereavement support, learning disability and dementia support
Patient Handling & Rescue
Full ambulance patient handling: motorised stretcher, carry chair, slide sheet, handling belt, Mangar Elk/Camel lifting cushions, Raizer II chair, tracked carry chair, orthopaedic stretcher. Light rescue in the pre-hospital environment. Recovery position (adult, child and infant). MILS.
Specialist Clinical
Newborn Life Support. Obstetrics & gynaecology presentations. Management of neurovascular emergencies. Abdominal examination. Airway emergencies in laryngectomy/tracheostomy patients. Chest seals. End of life and bereavement support. Learning disability and dementia support. Recognition of the sick child.

Dance & Performance Events
Dismounted EAC · Venue floor · Indoor, high footfall
In a national dance competition with hundreds of competitors and spectators, risk includes soft tissue injuries, cardiac events in older spectators, anaphylaxis, and medical emergencies in crowded enclosed space. I am on the floor, not in a van outside.
What I Do
  • Pre-event venue walk, risk assessment, evacuation route identification
  • Medical post setup, equipment and medication checks
  • On-floor patient assessment, treatment, documentation and handover
  • Liaison with event management and NWAS if escalation required

Road Races & Running Events
Vehicle-based EAC · Race route cover · Dynamic patient locations
In a 10K, the patient can be anywhere along several kilometres of route, often in areas with restricted vehicle access. The challenge is rapid response to a dynamic location. I know how to navigate it.
What I Do
  • Pre-race route survey, marshal locations, ambulance access routes
  • Vehicle patrol covering full route, monitoring radio and runner welfare
  • Rapid response: collapses, cardiac events, hypo/hyperthermia, trauma
  • Post-race finish line cover — statistically the highest-risk period

Recent: High Legh 10K, Cheshire — March 2026

Music & Festival Events
Crowd medical · High-density environment · Wide presentation range
Music events present a high-density crowd environment with a wide presentation range — intoxication, heat illness, crowd-crush injuries, and pre-existing conditions decompensating in an environment that makes assessment challenging.
What I Do
  • Medical post triage and management of walk-in and stretcher patients
  • Crowd patrol and welfare monitoring during high-density periods
  • Intoxication, heat illness, and emergency management
  • Coordination with security, event management and NWAS
🏢
Corporate & Community Events
Flexible deployment · Professional presence · Varied risk
Corporate and community events require a visible, professional medical presence that reassures attendees and organisers while remaining fully operationally ready.
What I Do
  • Professional uniformed medical presence throughout the event
  • Risk assessment and emergency action plan review with organisers
  • Response to any medical emergency to full EAC clinical standard
  • Post-event documentation and incident reporting as required
