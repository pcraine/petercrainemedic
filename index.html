Clinical Background

I am a practising Emergency Ambulance Crew (EAC) member with St John Ambulance North West, FREC 3 certified and currently progressing FREC 4 / Advanced Ambulance Practitioner. My clinical scope covers the full EAC column 9 of 10 — 19 medications including adrenaline IM, naloxone IM, salbutamol and ipratropium nebulisers, entonox, penthrox, GTN and aspirin — alongside comprehensive airway, trauma, assessment and monitoring capability. I am available for event medical cover across the North West.

I hold a full clinical deployment record: 32+ confirmed operational duties across sporting events, road races, large-scale public events, multi-agency exercises and community first aid. Clinical highlights include a nuclear site emergency planning exercise at Heysham NPP (5 simulated casualties, JESIP command huddles, Army EOD, fire, police and armed response); 25-patient Grand National deployment as zone lead clinician; suspected MI with 12-lead ECG acquisition and pre-alert to A&E; working at height trauma KED application at 25m with fire service extrication.

I maintain a full clinical log available on request.

"Every deployment runs to the same clinical standard — a sporting event crowd of fifty or a regional call with thousands present. The incident does not know the difference."

Peter Craine — EAC · FREC 3 · SJA North West

Clinical Reality

On Deployment

What actually happens when an EAC is deployed — the clinical standard behind every event.
FREC 3 EAC · 19 Medications · Full Airway, Trauma & Assessment Capability
Clinical Assessment
Full primary & secondary survey, cABCDE adult & paediatric, vital signs monitoring, NEWS2 early warning score, FAST stroke assessment, AMTS, GCS, pupil assessment, clinical decision-making to EAC standard
Airway & Breathing
OPA, NPA, iGel supraglottic airway, BVM ventilation (single & two-person), mechanical & manual suction, oxygen therapy, capnography, airway emergency management including laryngectomy/tracheostomy patients
Medications
19 medications within EAC scope — adrenaline IM, naloxone IM, salbutamol NEB, ipratropium NEB, entonox INH, penthrox INH, hydrocortisone IM, glucagon IM, GTN spray SL, aspirin PO, paracetamol PO, ibuprofen PO, glucose gel PO, loratadine PO, ORS PO, oxygen INH, plus three patient-own medications
Trauma & Haemorrhage
Haemorrhage control, wound packing, tourniquet application (CAT x2 in kit), chest seal, haemostatic gauze, spinal immobilisation (KED, scoop stretcher, long board, spider harness), extrication techniques, major trauma management
Monitoring
12-lead ECG acquisition and interpretation, pre-alert to PPCI/stroke/A&E pathways, pulse oximetry, NIBP, glucometry, capnography, temperature monitoring
Documentation
Patient Report Forms (PRFs), cardiac arrest documentation, mass casualty triage documentation, ATMIST/SBAR/ASHICE handover, clinical records management to SJA and CQC standards
🏃
Road Races & Running Events
Vehicle EAC · Dismounted · Treatment centre
Running events produce a predictable range of presentations: exertional illness, musculoskeletal injury, hypoglycaemia, exercise-induced asthma and cardiac events. The challenge is triage at volume — multiple concurrent contacts, remote course locations and time-sensitive decisions on escalation to NWAS.
What I Do
  • Pre-event course walk, hazard identification, evacuation planning
  • Treatment centre setup, equipment checks, DIPS review
  • Mobile response on course, patient assessment and treatment at point of collapse
  • Triage, documentation and NWAS escalation when required

Recent: Darwen Roller Coaster Half Marathon — April 2026. High Legh 10K — March 2026.

💃
Dance & Performance Events
Dismounted EAC · Venue floor · Indoor, high footfall
Large-scale dance competitions and performance events. Risk profile includes soft tissue injuries in competitors, cardiac events in older spectators, anaphylaxis and medical emergencies in crowded enclosed spaces. On-floor clinical cover — not a van parked outside.
What I Do
  • Pre-event venue walk, risk assessment, evacuation route identification
  • Medical post setup, equipment and medication checks
  • On-floor patient assessment, treatment, documentation and handover
  • Liaison with event management and NWAS if escalation required

Recent: IDTA Nationwide Solo Dance Festival, Norbreck Castle, Blackpool — March 2026.

🏄
Football & Rugby Fixtures
Dismounted EAC · Crowd & player cover · Pitch-side
Dual-role cover for sports fixtures: crowd medical cover and player/staff clinical support. Cold-weather contact sport presents musculoskeletal injury, concussion, facial trauma and hypothermia. Crowd contacts include cardiac events, falls and alcohol-related presentations.
What I Do
  • Pitch-side and crowd station coverage throughout fixture
  • Player assessment, pitch-side treatment and return-to-play decisions in consultation with team staff
  • Crowd patient assessment, treatment and NWAS referral where required
  • Post-match documentation and debrief

Recent: AFC Fylde v AFC Marine (dual role, no team doctor) — March 2026. DW Stadium rugby, Wigan — March 2026.

🏈
Major Race Events
Zone Lead · Mass gathering · Multi-agency
Large-scale race events with thousands of spectators and multi-zone clinical coverage. The Grand National operates at a different scale to most events — 25 patients in a single deployment, diverse presentations including alcohol-related illness, hypovolaemia, airway compromise and complex stoma care. Zone lead responsibility throughout.
What I Do
  • Zone lead clinician responsibility — all patient contacts within designated area
  • Triage, assessment, treatment and documentation for all presentations
  • NWAS liaison, handover and escalation for P1/P2 patients
  • Multi-agency coordination with race medical team and security

Recent: Grand National, Aintree Racecourse — 25 patients, zone lead — April 2026.

Exercise EAC · JESIP · Command participation
Full-scale emergency planning exercises alongside fire, police, armed response and specialist agencies. Clinical skills operate identically whether casualties are real or simulated — triage, haemorrhage control, tourniquet application, ATMIST handover and PRF documentation. JESIP command participation as clinical voice.
What I Do
  • Full clinical response to simulated casualties: triage, assessment, treatment, documentation
  • JESIP command huddle participation as clinical representative
  • Multi-agency coordination across fire, police, EOD and armed response
  • Hot debrief and after-action review contribution

Recent: Heysham NPP multi-agency emergency planning exercise — 5 casualties, Army EOD, JESIP — April 2026.
