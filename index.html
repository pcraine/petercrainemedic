Clinical Background

I am a practising Emergency Ambulance Crew (EAC) member with St John Ambulance North West, FREC 3 certified and currently progressing FREC 4 / Advanced Ambulance Practitioner. My clinical scope covers the full EAC column 9 of 10 — 19 medications including adrenaline IM, naloxone IM, salbutamol and ipratropium nebulisers, entonox, penthrox, GTN and aspirin — alongside comprehensive airway, trauma, assessment and monitoring capability. I am available for event medical cover across the North West.

I hold a full clinical deployment record: 32+ confirmed operational duties across sporting events, road races, large-scale public events, multi-agency exercises and community first aid. Clinical highlights include a nuclear site emergency planning exercise at Heysham NPP (5 simulated casualties, JESIP command huddles, Army EOD, fire, police and armed response); 25-patient Grand National deployment as zone lead clinician; suspected MI with 12-lead ECG acquisition and pre-alert to A&E; working at height trauma KED application at 25m with fire service extrication.

I maintain a full clinical log available on request.

For Event Organisers
Does your event need EAC-level medical cover?
Expected attendance over 500 people
Physical activity — running, cycling, sport, combat or physical exertion
Alcohol or substance exposure is likely
Remote location or extended NWAS response time
Council, licensing authority or insurer requires a documented medical plan
If any of the above apply, a standard first aider is not sufficient. EAC-level cover closes the clinical gap between a first aid box and a 999 call — with medications, equipment and trained decision-making on site.
FREC 3 EAC · 19 Medications · Full Airway, Trauma & Assessment Capability
Clinical Assessment
Full primary & secondary survey, cABCDE adult & paediatric, vital signs monitoring, NEWS2 early warning score, FAST stroke assessment, AMTS, GCS, pupil assessment, clinical decision-making to EAC standard
Airway & Breathing
OPA, NPA, iGel supraglottic airway, BVM ventilation (single & two-person), mechanical & manual suction, oxygen therapy, capnography, airway emergency management including laryngectomy/tracheostomy patients
Medications
19 medications within EAC scope — adrenaline IM, naloxone IM, salbutamol NEB, ipratropium NEB, entonox INH, penthrox INH, hydrocortisone IM, glucagon IM, GTN spray SL, aspirin PO, paracetamol PO, ibuprofen PO, glucose gel PO, loratadine PO, ORS PO, oxygen INH, plus three patient-own medications
Trauma & Haemorrhage
Haemorrhage control, wound packing, tourniquet application (CAT x2 in kit), chest seal, haemostatic gauze, spinal immobilisation (KED, scoop stretcher, long board, spider harness), extrication techniques, major trauma management
Monitoring
12-lead ECG acquisition and interpretation, pre-alert to PPCI/stroke/A&E pathways, pulse oximetry, NIBP, glucometry, capnography, temperature monitoring
Documentation
Patient Report Forms (PRFs), cardiac arrest documentation, mass casualty triage documentation, ATMIST/SBAR/ASHICE handover, clinical records management to SJA and CQC standards
Assessing your patients
Full head-to-toe clinical assessment of any patient at your event — from a twisted ankle to a cardiac arrest. Proper clinical decision-making at point of contact, including when to escalate to 999 and what to tell them.
Keeping airways open
Equipment and skills to keep an unconscious patient breathing while an ambulance responds — including oxygen, suction and airway devices a standard first aider cannot use or carry.
19 medications on site
Adrenaline for anaphylaxis. Naloxone for opioid emergencies. Pain relief that works. Asthma treatment. Cardiac medications. Not “call 999 and wait.” Treatment starts at your event.
Managing trauma
Serious bleeding control, tourniquet application, wound packing and spinal management — everything needed to stabilise a seriously injured person and hand them over safely to NWAS.
Heart monitoring
A 12-lead ECG machine — the same device used in A&E — at your event. Peter can acquire and interpret it. If someone is having a heart attack, the hospital is pre-alerted before the ambulance leaves your site.
Documentation you can use
Every patient contact documented. Every medication recorded. Every NWAS handover formally logged. Written post-event medical report within 48 hours — suitable for insurance, licensing and council compliance.

What to ask any event medical provider
▼
Qualifications & Governance
✓
What is their clinical qualification level? First aid, FREC 3 or paramedic? FREC 3 EAC carries and administers 19 medications; a standard first aider carries none.
✓
Do they hold an Enhanced DBS certificate? Essential for any event with minors or vulnerable adults.
✓
Are they operating under a clinical governance framework? Who is clinically responsible for their decisions?
On The Day
✓
What medications will they carry? An EAC carries 19 including adrenaline, naloxone, pain relief and cardiac medications.
✓
Can they acquire a 12-lead ECG? Cardiac events are the leading cause of event fatality. Early ECG and pre-alert changes outcomes.
✓
What is their NWAS escalation protocol? How do they hand over to an ambulance crew, and is it formally documented?
Documentation
✓
Do they provide a post-event medical report? You may need this for insurance, licensing or council requirements. Many providers don’t include it.
✓
Do they complete Patient Report Forms for every contact? Clinical documentation is a governance requirement, not optional.
